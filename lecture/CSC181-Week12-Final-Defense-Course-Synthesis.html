Defense day

Before you walk in

The product

  • v1.0.0 deployed and smoke-tested this morning
  • Demo accounts signed in or ready
  • Seed data reset to the demo story
  • Phone hotspot ready as a fallback

The presentation

  • Slides as PDF on two devices and a USB drive
  • Backup demo video opened, not just saved
  • Notifications and auto-updates off
  • Font size and zoom set for the room

The team

  • Who says what, and who drives the demo
  • Who answers which area first
  • The repository open, ready to show evidence
  • Water, a timer, and a calm start

Most demo failures happen before the demo starts: expired sessions, a sleeping laptop, a captive Wi-Fi page.

06

Defense day

Handling the hard moments

When this happensDo this
The demo breaksSay so calmly, switch to the backup video or screenshots, and keep telling the story.
You do not know the answer"I'm not sure. Here is how I would find out." Do not guess or bluff.
A teammate is stuckLet them finish their thought, then add briefly: "To add to that…". Do not take over.
You are running over timeSkip to the demo and results. Cut detail, never the demo.
A panelist points out a flawThank them, acknowledge it, say what you would change. It is not an attack.
A question is about someone else's partAnswer what you know about how it fits the system, then hand over.

Course synthesis

What software engineering is

Definition (IEEE)

The application of a systematic, disciplined, quantifiable approach to the development, operation, and maintenance of software.

Systematic

A process you can explain: sprints, reviews, tests, releases.

Disciplined

You followed it under pressure: the freeze, the PRs, the tests.

Quantifiable

You measured: NFRs, velocity, coverage, defects, estimates versus actuals.

ISO/IEC/IEEE 24765:2017, Systems and software engineering: Vocabulary

Course synthesis

Six ideas that outlast the tools

1

Understand before you build

Stakeholders and the real problem come first

2

Make requirements testable

If you cannot check it, you cannot deliver it

3

Design for change

Cohesion, low coupling, and recorded decisions

4

Small, reviewed, integrated steps

Thin slices, small PRs, merge daily

5

Build quality in

Tests, reviews, and security from the start, not the end

6

Software is made by people

Communicate, give feedback, and share the work

Frameworks and tools will change many times in your career. These ideas have held for decades.

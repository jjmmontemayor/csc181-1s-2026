03

Scrum roles

Who does what on your team

Product owner

  • Orders the backlog and keeps it current
  • Speaks for the stakeholders you interviewed
  • Accepts or rejects a story at the review
  • Says "not this sprint" to new requests

Scrum master

  • Runs planning, stand-ups, review, and retro
  • Chases blockers; keeps the board honest
  • Posts the stand-up summary
  • Rotates each sprint, so two people do it

Developers

  • Everyone on the team, including the PO and SM
  • Pick tasks, branch, commit, open pull requests
  • Review each other's code within 24 hours
  • Move their own cards on the board

In a team of four, the product owner and scrum master also write code. They are accountabilities, not job titles.

06

Sprint planning

A 60-minute sprint planning meeting

5 min

The product owner shows the top of the backlog and proposes a goal.

10 min

Agree the sprint goal in one sentence.

10 min

Pull stories from the top until the goal is covered.

20 min

Split each story into tasks of a day or less.

10 min

Check the tasks against capacity. Drop or split if over.

5 min

Give each story an owner and a reviewer; set the Iteration field.

Pull, do not push

The developers decide how much fits. The product owner decides the order.

Output

A sprint goal, a set of stories on the board marked Sprint 1, and tasks with owners.

K. Schwaber & J. Sutherland, The Scrum Guide (2020)

10

During the sprint

Stand-ups: three questions, three times a week

Useful

Ben · Wed 8:40 PM

Done: Pickup slot picker (#2), PR #14 open.

Next: Save the order to the database.

Blocked: Need Ana to review #14 before I can merge.

Not useful

Dan · Wed 11:58 PM

Done: Worked on the app.

Next: Continue.

Blocked: None.

When

Mon, Wed, Fri: 10 minutes on a call, or posted in the team chat by 9 PM.

Rule

Name the issue number. Say what you will finish next, not what you will "work on".

Scrum master

Reads every post, then follows up on each blocker the same day.

K. Schwaber & J. Sutherland, The Scrum Guide (2020)

During the sprint

Warning signs, and what to do

You noticeWhat it usually meansWhat to do
A card in In progress for three daysThe task is too big or blockedSplit it, or pair on it today
Pull requests waiting two daysEveryone is building; no one is reviewingStop starting; everyone reviews first
A stakeholder asks for a new featureNormal; ideas keep comingAdd it to the backlog. The PO orders it; the sprint stays.
A member misses two stand-upsOverloaded, stuck, or checked outScrum master asks privately; share the work; tell the instructor if it continues
Half the sprint gone, little in DoneBehind planCut scope now. Keep the goal; drop the stretch.

13

During the sprint

Mid-sprint backlog refinement: 30 minutes

1

Check the board

Is the goal still reachable? Cut or split now if not.

2

Look ahead

Read the next five stories in the backlog together.

3

Clarify

Rewrite unclear acceptance criteria; ask a stakeholder if needed.

4

Split and size

Split anything L; size anything new.

5

Re-order

The product owner adds feedback and new requests in the right place.

Refinement makes the next sprint planning fast. It is not a second planning meeting.

14

End of the sprint

The sprint review: show, then adapt

1

Goal

Read the sprint goal. Met, partly met, or not met?

2

Live demo

Show working software from main. Every member drives part of it.

3

Done and not done

Which stories meet the Definition of Done; the rest go back to the backlog.

4

Board

Show the board as it is. Explain flat stretches honestly.

5

Feedback

Write every comment down as a new issue.

6

Next

Re-order the backlog for the next sprint.

K. Schwaber & J. Sutherland, The Scrum Guide (2020)

15

End of the sprint

The retrospective: 20 minutes, one or two changes

2 min

Read the Prime Directive aloud.

6 min

Each person writes sticky notes: Start, Stop, Continue.

6 min

Group similar notes; vote with three dots each.

6 min

Turn the top one or two into actions with an owner.

Prime Directive

Regardless of what we discover, we understand and truly believe that everyone did the best job they could, given what they knew at the time, their skills and abilities, the resources available, and the situation at hand.

Output

Write the actions into the working agreement. Check them at the next retro.

N. Kerth, Project Retrospectives (2001) · E. Derby & D. Larsen, Agile Retrospectives (2006)

16

Sprint 2

Sprint 2 is a finishing sprint

Plan with evidence

  • Use what you finished in Sprint 1 (velocity), not hopes
  • Unfinished Sprint 1 stories come first if still Must
  • Add the review feedback, ordered by the PO

Protect the finish

  • Feature freeze on Wed Dec 9
  • Dec 9–11: fix bugs, test, write docs
  • Every Must-have story meets the Definition of Done

Prepare the demo

  • Sprint 2 review on Fri Dec 11 is the dress rehearsal
  • Dec 12–13: fix only what the rehearsal broke
  • Mon Dec 14: demo day

A small MVP that works in the demo beats a large one that crashes. Cut scope before the freeze, not after.

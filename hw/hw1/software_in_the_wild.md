# CSC181 Week 1 Activity

## Software in the Wild: Improving the Diyandi Experience Through Software

## Overview

Diyandi Festival in Iligan is a local celebration that brings together events, communities, performers, vendors, visitors, organizers, and residents. A festival like this creates many opportunities to think about how software can help people find information, move safely, coordinate activities, communicate updates, access services, and enjoy events more conveniently.

For this activity, you will imagine a digital system that could improve **one part of the Diyandi experience**.

> You do **not** need to build the system. You also do **not** need to know formal software-engineering terms yet. Focus on understanding a user’s need, describing a useful idea, and explaining how it could help.

---

## Learning goals

After completing this activity, you should be able to:

- Identify a group of users and a need they may have
- Describe a concrete problem or inconvenience in a local context
- Propose a practical digital solution
- Identify actions users should be able to perform using the proposed system
- Recognize qualities that can make a digital solution useful and trustworthy
- Explain how you could determine whether a proposed solution helped users

## Design respectfully

Diyandi represents the cultures, traditions, faith, and communities of Iligan. Design your proposed solution respectfully, inclusively, and practically.

- Do not treat cultural or religious practices merely as entertainment or a software feature.
- Avoid stereotypes, disrespectful descriptions, or inappropriate use of cultural symbols.
- Consider users with different needs, including residents, visitors, seniors, persons with disabilities, parents, vendors, performers, and people with limited internet access.
- Do not assume that every user has a high-end phone, unlimited mobile data, stable internet, or strong digital skills.
- If your idea involves user data, location, payments, photographs, or incident reports, consider privacy and safety.

## Example: Diyandi event guide

**User group:** Visitors and Iligan residents who want to attend Diyandi events.

**Situation or need:** Users want to know what events are happening, where they are located, what time they begin, and whether schedules have changed.

**Problem:** Festival information may be spread across posters, social-media pages, announcements, and word of mouth. A visitor may miss an event, go to the wrong venue, or not learn about a schedule change in time.

**Proposed digital solution:** A mobile-friendly Diyandi Event Guide that shows the official schedule, venue map, event categories, public advisories, and announcements.

**Things users can do:**

1. Search or filter events by date, category, venue, or intended audience.
2. View directions and receive notifications if an event’s venue or time changes.

**Important qualities:**

- The guide should be easy to use because people may need information quickly while moving between venues.
- The guide should work with limited mobile data because not every attendee will have stable or fast internet access.

**How to tell whether it helped:** Ask users whether they found events more easily, observe whether users can locate selected venues, and collect feedback about schedule accuracy and ease of use.

Focus on thinking carefully about people, local context, and how software can help.
